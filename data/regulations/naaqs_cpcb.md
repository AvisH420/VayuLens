# National Ambient Air Quality Standards (NAAQS) and the CPCB AQI

The Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) sets the National Ambient Air
Quality Standards and publishes the National Air Quality Index.

## Key standards (24-hour average, residential/industrial)
- PM2.5: 60 µg/m³
- PM10: 100 µg/m³
- NO2: 80 µg/m³
- SO2: 80 µg/m³
- CO: 2 mg/m³ (1-hour: 4 mg/m³)
- Ozone (O3): 100 µg/m³ (8-hour)

Annual standards are lower — PM2.5 40 µg/m³ and PM10 60 µg/m³.

## CPCB Air Quality Index bands (based on PM2.5, PM10, and other pollutants)
- Good: AQI 0–50 — minimal impact.
- Satisfactory: AQI 51–100 — minor breathing discomfort to sensitive people.
- Moderate: AQI 101–200 — breathing discomfort to people with lung/heart disease.
- Poor: AQI 201–300 — breathing discomfort to most people on prolonged exposure.
- Very Poor: AQI 301–400 — respiratory illness on prolonged exposure.
- Severe: AQI 401–500 — affects healthy people and seriously impacts those with
  existing diseases; avoid all outdoor activity.

The AQI is computed from sub-indices for each pollutant; the overall AQI is the
worst (maximum) sub-index, provided at least three pollutants are monitored with
one being PM2.5 or PM10.

## Health guidance by band
- Poor and above: sensitive groups (children, elderly, pregnant people, and
  those with heart or lung conditions) should avoid outdoor exertion.
- Very Poor / Severe: everyone should minimise outdoor activity, keep windows
  closed during peak hours, and use N95 masks outdoors where feasible.
